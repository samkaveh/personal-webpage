Selected work — 2013 → 2026

Four chapters, one engineering discipline.

The domains vary — autonomous agents, enterprise supply chains, real-time graphics, and high-throughput data pipelines — but the philosophy remains constant: understand the system deeply, isolate the leverage points, and build software that is simple, deterministic, and observable.

01 — Agentic systems

Customer-Facing Agentic MLOps

Lead ArchitectAgentic AIA2A ProtocolsMCP ToolsRAGBillions of RowsEvals & Gates

As lead architect and developer at Blue Yonder, I designed and built a customer-facing agent for data science workflows, machine learning model engineering, and MLOps in the demand forecasting domain. The agent is extensively used during customer onboarding and migration to our microservices platform, enabling enterprise teams to analyze data, improve machine learning models, and investigate model behavior using deterministic evidence rather than opaque heuristics.

To make the platform an extensible foundation across the company, I led the integration of Agent-to-Agent (A2A) protocols and developer-focused interfaces based on the Model Context Protocol (MCP). This allows other teams across Blue Yonder to integrate their domain services directly into the agent runtime, backed by Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) architectures grounded in internal knowledge bases and codebases.

The system integrates directly with an event-driven query and analytics engine capable of performing aggregations over datasets containing billions of rows, empowering operators with rapid, data-driven decision-making. Furthermore, we extended our MLOps services to support in-pipeline data transformations, enabling what-if scenarios without rerunning the full data-ingestion chain while maintaining rigorous transformation history and provenance.

To ensure production reliability across enterprise deployments, I implemented human-in-the-loop workflows, interactive visualization artifacts, automated agent evaluations, and CI/CD release gates. Today, I own and operate these services across multiple global regions, managing deployment, monitoring, and day-to-day production operations.

02 — Forecasting

Sustainable supply chains

ForecastingPricingEvent enginesCloud

At Blue Yonder I develop services that power automated replenishment, multi-horizon demand forecasting, and dynamic pricing for global retail enterprises. Designing an event-driven query engine capable of running analytical aggregations across datasets with billions of rows allows operators to make high-stakes inventory decisions backed by empirical data.

By aligning demand predictions closely with store orders, the system reduces overproduction and perishables spoilage — cutting environmental waste while preserving customer availability. The leverage is rarely in the machine learning model alone; it is in the closed loop of signal, decision, and feedback.

03 — Entrepreneurship

Building virtual skies

FoundingGraphicsSystemsC++

As a startup co-founder and software engineer, I built projection-mapping software and hardware systems for full-dome theatres and spherical displays. The software I developed solves non-linear optical distortion and multi-projector edge blending, powering immersive installations worldwide.

Building a venture from the ground up provided a complete lesson in end-to-end software ownership: writing performance-critical graphics code in C++, working directly with clients on technical integrations, and resolving field escalations under real-world deadlines.

04 — Scientific ML

Data pipelines at scale

PipelinesDeep learningHPCCERN

During my doctoral research at CERN and DESY, I applied machine learning methods to analyze fundamental particle interactions and designed data pipelines for ingestion, cleaning, and feature engineering across multi-petabyte datasets produced by particle detectors.

The challenge centered on extracting extremely rare statistical signatures from massive background noise using deep learning classifiers on distributed HPC clusters. This background in rigorous experiment design, statistical validation, and reproducible data pipelines continues to directly inform how I architect production AI systems today.
